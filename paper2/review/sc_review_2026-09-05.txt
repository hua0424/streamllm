DA — STRONGEST COUNTERARGUMENT
The strongest skeptical reading is that the paper tests a carefully wrapped cache-slicing primitive against another slice of the same tensor, then verifies that identical retained states remain equal under identical operations. Those are useful regression tests, but their success alone is not evidence that a spoken-dialogue system behaves better. The playback boundary is constructed from software fragments rather than acoustic alignment. In the difficult partial-fragment case, it deliberately retains content that the software cursor has not consumed. A valid state can therefore still represent a policy-dependent approximation of the interaction rather than the listener's history. The only downstream comparison is uncertain under fixed automated detectors, and the candidate study uses a synchronous oracle rather than a real end-of-speech decision. Consequently the reported cost advantage may motivate prefix reuse without showing that this particular repair contract is necessary, sufficient, or beneficial in a deployed speech loop.
This does not make the exact checks vacuous: masks, ledgers, role/EOT state and independently derived lengths can fail even when crop itself is simple. The warranted contribution is an inspectable coupled-state implementation and an auditable finite test protocol. The editorial question is whether that bounded contribution has sufficient generalizable significance for a full-length speech journal article. A more ambitious answer requires additional evidence, not a longer caveat section.
DA-1 [major significance concern]: Sections 4.4/5.2/6.1 need to distinguish coupled-state assurance from same-input tautology and whole-system correctness. Minimum remedy is precise assurance scope; stronger remedy is a separately designed external-system evaluation, outside authorization.
DA-2 [minor overstatement]: Introduction 'specific to interactive speech systems' is too exclusive for a contract explicitly motivated by external progress; analogous speculative/buffered systems exist. Change to 'arises in interactive speech systems'.
